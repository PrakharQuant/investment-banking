01What Investment Banking Actually Is

An investment bank is an intermediary and advisor: it connects companies that need capital with investors who have it, and advises on transformational transactions. It does not trade its own money as its core business (that's the sales & trading desk). The two revenue engines are:

Advisory

Selling a company, buying one, merging two, taking one private or public, defending against a hostile raid. Fee-for-service, margin-rich, brand-driven. Think M&A and restructuring.

Underwriting / Capital Markets

Raising debt (DCM) or equity (ECM): IPOs, secondaries, corporate bonds, leveraged loans. Banks commit risk (firm-commitment deals) or simply distribute (best-efforts), earning spread/fees on billions of face value.

One mental model: bankers monetise events in a company's life — founding (venture), growth (debt/equity raises), change of control (M&A), distress (restructuring), succession (private wealth). Each event has a product group attached to it.

02How a Bank Is Organised

Inside IBD, teams split along two axes: Coverage (relationship owners, organised by industry or by client type) and Products (technical deal specialists, organised by transaction type). You staff a deal as Coverage + Industry + Product.

Industry Coverage

TMT, Healthcare, Industrials, Consumer/Retail, Energy & Power, Financial Institutions (FIG), Real Estate. You own the client relationship and originate ideas.

Geographic / Client Coverage

Global Clients / Large Corporates, Mid-Market / Regional, Sponsor Coverage (PE firms as clients), Ventures & Origins (startups).

M&A Product

Financial sponsors (Sponsor M&A / LBO), general strategic M&A, take-privates. Valuation, fairness opinions, merger models.

Leveraged Finance

Structuring and distributing debt for LBOs: term loans, high-yield bonds, unitranche. Where credit analysis meets deal-making.

ECM / DCM

Equity Capital Markets runs IPOs, follow-ons, convertibles; Debt Capital Markets runs investment-grade bonds and liability management. Execution-heavy, calendar-driven.

Restructuring & SPACs

Distressed advisory, DIP financing, pre-packaged bankruptcies; sponsor-led acquisition vehicles. Counter-cyclical franchises that boom when others shrink.

Adjacent desks you'll work with constantly: Equity Research (sector views, "wall-crossing"), Sales & Trading (distribution, block trades), Wealth Management (selling your deals to UHNW allocators), and Legal / Compliance (the reason everything takes longer than you think).

07Career Path: Analyst to Managing Director

Years 1–2 · Analyst

The grind & the gateway

Recruited out of undergrad (or a pre-experience master's). Excel factory floor: comps, precedents, due diligence lists, 2 a.m. revisions. Up-or-out: ~70–80% leave after two years — deliberately.

Years 3–5 · Associate

Own the mechanics

Promoted or MBA-hired. You now run working sessions, sanity-check every number leaving the team, and start drafting process letters and information memoranda.

Years 6–9 · Vice President

Run the process

Multiple live deals simultaneously. You interface with management teams, coordinate legal/accounting workstreams, and keep the MD's client promises on schedule.

Years 10–13 · Director / ED

Start carrying a book

Hybrid role: execution leadership plus targeted marketing. Promotion to MD is gated on demonstrated origination, not effort.

Year 14+ · Managing Director

Sell, then advise

Your job is trust: CEOs, boards, PE principals calling you. Compensation tracks fees you generate. Below the MD line banks pay for work; above it they pay for relationships.

08Exit Opportunities

IB is famously a two-year launchpad. The standard destinations, and why banking trains you for them:

Private Equity

The marquee exit. LBO modelling, due-diligence discipline and deal exposure translate directly. Mega-funds (Blackstone, KKR, Apollo, Carlyle) recruit ex-BB/EB analysts heavily.

Hedge Funds

Fundamental long/short shops value your sector knowledge and valuation chops; activist funds (Elliott-type) love the legal-process literacy from contested deals.

Corporate Development / Strategy

In-house M&A teams at corporates: screening targets, running sell-side carve-outs, integration planning. Better hours, same skillset.

Venture Capital & Growth Equity

Especially after tech/TMT coverage or fintech product stints; sourcing, term sheets, cap-table and structure fluency carry over.

Business School

Top MBA programmes treat IB analyst stints as gold-standard work experience; the classic Associate-via-MBA re-entry path.

Entrepreneurship / Operating Roles

You've watched hundreds of owners sell businesses; many bankers conclude they'd rather build one. Deal-network access is the hidden asset.

09Breaking In

Routes in

① Target-school undergraduate recruiting (spring "off-cycle" internships → summer analyst → return offer). ② Post-experience master's / finance masters. ③ MBA into Associate. ④ Lateral moves from Big-4 deal advisory, restructuring or corporate development. ⑤ Off-target hustle: networking so hard it becomes a credential.

Interview essentials

Technical: three-statement linkages, DCF mechanics, accretion/dilution, LBO walk-through, why EV not equity value. Behavioral: why banking, why this group, a leadership-under-pressure story. Market awareness: pitch a live idea — buyer, seller, rationale, price.

Reality check: the hours are brutal and the work is often unglamorous, but the density of learning, the brand durability, and the optionality on exit make it one of the highest-leverage first careers in finance.
